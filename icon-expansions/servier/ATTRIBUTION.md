**Source:** [Servier Medical Art](https://smart.servier.com) (SMART), the medical illustration bank of Servier. The kits
name no creator per drawing; the credit goes to Servier Medical Art.

under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0", printed on every slide of the kits; the same in the site's terms of use). See
`LICENSE.txt`.

**How Servier Medical Art asks to be credited** (read on https://smart.servier.com/using-smart-images-what-you-can-do,
"How do I give credit for SMART images?", on 2026-11-12):

> Image adapted from Servier Medical Art (https://smart.servier.com/), licensed under CC BY 4.0

(for a modified image; "Image provided by …" for one used as it is). Its terms of use
(https://smart.servier.com/terms-of-use/) add the link to the license and that the credit must not suggest that Servier
endorses you or your use. Every icon here was converted, so the line SiliCoDraw writes on the page is: *Images adapted
from Servier Medical Art (https://smart.servier.com/), licensed under CC BY 4.0
(https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)*.

**Not part of the license:** Servier's name, trademarks and logos (none of them is in this pack; the logo of the kits
is on their slide layouts, which the converter never reads), and the website itself. SiliCoDraw is not affiliated with
or endorsed by Servier. Servier's FAQ asks that apps using the images do not market them "as a standalone image library
or collection": this pack is an optional part of SiliCoDraw, imported from inside the editor after its terms were
shown, and is not offered on its own.

**Where the drawings come from:** `ServierMedicalArt-all-kits.zip`, the one ZIP of all 49 PowerPoint kits that the site
offers (downloaded by hand on 2026-11-12; the site forbids automated downloads and has no SVG files). One category per
kit.

**What SiliCoDraw changed:** every drawing of every slide (a group, a freeform shape or an SVG picture) was redrawn as
SVG from the kit's own vector shapes with `dev/icon-import` (`pptx.js`, then `convert.js`): fitted into a 100 × 100 box,
coordinates rounded to a tenth of a unit, text labels left out (a caption under, above or beside a drawing gives its name, for drawings the slides do
not caption a name from `itemNames` in `packs/servier.json` (pieces of clothing, countries), otherwise the slide title; drawings of one slide with one name are told apart by their main color, then by a number), parts smaller
than half a pixel at 300 px dropped, loose single shapes lying on a drawing (granules, dots) joined to it, and the main
color and the accent made recolorable together with their shades. Servier's shaded style (shading and highlights drawn
as shapes of other shades) is kept. Names are in American spelling; the search words keep the kits' spelling too.

**Colorways:** 235 drawings that are the same shapes as another drawing of this pack in other colors are no icons of their own: they are colorways of 109 icons (the icon in their colors, one click away in SiliCoDraw's side panel; their names still find it, and figures that used them keep drawing them). The drawings themselves are unchanged. A drawing that shows another state of a thing is an icon of its own, not a colorway (the four traffic lights, each with another lamp lit).

**Left out:** raster pictures and groups that hold one (photographs, scans, pictures without vector shapes), plain shapes
on their own (leader lines, arrows, frames), items drawn with lines only (curved arrows, loose strokes, bare outlines),
drawings painted only in white (invisible on a light page), items whose main content is text, the title and notice
slides, drawings that look exactly the same as one already in the pack (compared pixel by pixel; the dropped copy's name
stays a search word of the kept one), and drawings still over 300 KB after conversion. The numbers per
