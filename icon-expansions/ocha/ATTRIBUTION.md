# OCHA Humanitarian Icons: attribution

**Source:** [OCHA Humanitarian Icons](https://un-ocha.github.io/humanitarian-icons/), published by the United Nations
Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) in its GitHub repository
[UN-OCHA/humanitarian-icons](https://github.com/UN-OCHA/humanitarian-icons) at commit
`f7613d5a6911fdbafa9706254cb42e2875b78069` (2026-02-12). The repository names no creator per icon, so this pack names
none.

**License:** public domain, dedicated under CC0 1.0 Universal ("The **Humanitarian Icons** are in the **Public
Domain**. You are free to: **Copy**, **modify**, and **distribute** the icons without requesting permission", the
repository's README; its LICENSE is the CC0 1.0 legal code). See `LICENSE.txt`.

**How OCHA asks to be credited** (the repository's README, read on 2026-11-14):

> While attribution is not required, we would appreciate:
> - Crediting **OCHA** where feasible.
> - Sending a notification to ochavisual@un.org with a link to your webpage or publication, for our records.

So no credit is required, and SiliCoDraw adds no credit line for these icons. Page → Copy full icon credits gives a
line for anyone who wants to credit OCHA: *OCHA Humanitarian Icons (un-ocha.github.io/humanitarian-icons), CC0 1.0*.
The notification is for you to send if you wish; SiliCoDraw never sends anything.

**No endorsement:** the README says: "OCHA does not intend to establish an official meaning or endorsement for each
Humanitarian Icon. Their meaning and representation do not imply acceptance or endorsement by the United Nations."
The names in this pack are SiliCoDraw's reading of the file names, not official meanings. The icons that carry UN
lettering (a UN compound, a UN vehicle, a peacekeeper's helmet) are not in the pack, and it contains no UN emblem: the
name and emblem of the United Nations are protected separately from copyright.

**What SiliCoDraw changed:** the SVG files were converted to SiliCoDraw's icon format with `dev/icon-import`, one color
version only (black; the repository's UN-blue and white files are the same drawings, and SiliCoDraw recolors icons).
Each icon was fitted into a 100 × 100 box, its style sheet written out as attributes, its coordinates rounded and its one
color made recolorable. Every icon was drawn and compared with the original on a white and on a dark page; all passed.
Names are the file names in words, in American spelling ("Humanitarian Program Cycle"; the file name stays the title
and a search word), with a typo corrected ("Coordinated Assessment") and a few abbreviations spelled out ("IDP and
Refugee Camp"); a few common words were added as search words (bacterium, hurricane, syringe). The repository has no
categories, so the groups in the Add panel (natural hazards, health, water and sanitation, food and agriculture,
people, shelter and buildings, infrastructure and transport, communication and technology, data and coordination) are
SiliCoDraw's.

**Left out (104 of the 387 icons):** user-interface and file-type symbols (arrows and chevrons, add, remove, edit, save,
copy, share, search, settings, menus, folders, documents, mail, chat, user accounts, play and pause, PDF, CSV, DOCX,
XLSX and ZIP files, and similar); the plain status marks (a check, a cross and an exclamation mark in a circle);
icons whose meaning is lettering (NGO office, NFI); icons of conflict, violence and the military (abduction, arrest,
assault, attack, carjacking, a checkpoint, a child combatant, conflict, forced entry and recruitment, gender-based and
sexual violence, harassment, a military airport and gate, a landmine, murder, a national army, a rebel, robbery, and
civil-military coordination); religious buildings (a Buddhist temple, a church, a Hindu temple, a mosque); and the
icons with UN lettering (UN compound office, UN vehicle, peacekeeping force).
